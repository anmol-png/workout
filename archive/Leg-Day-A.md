## The principles this plan runs on (read once — applies everywhere)

1. **Progressive overload drives growth.** Every exercise uses **double progression**: you
   have a rep range (e.g. 6–8). Start at the bottom. When you hit the **top of the range on
   all sets at the target RPE**, add the smallest load increment next session and drop back to
   the bottom. Safest, most reliable intermediate method.

2. **RPE (Rate of Perceived Exertion) = reps left in the tank.**
   - RPE 10 = failure (0 left) · RPE 9 = 1 left · RPE 8 = 2 left · RPE 7 = 3 left.
   - We train **RPE 7–9**: hard enough to grow, short of grinding failure that wrecks recovery
     (matters at 6–7h sleep). Machines/isolation go closer to failure (safe); barbell lifts
     stay 1–3 reps shy (protects technique + joints).

3. **Order = hardest/most-skilled first, while fresh.** Squat leads (most technical +
   fatiguing). Isolation and machine work follows, when fatigue won't compromise safety.

4. **Rep-range logic:**
   - **5–8** (squat) → strength + dense muscle, builds the lagging squat.
   - **8–12** (RDL, split squat, leg press) → prime hypertrophy zone, best size-per-effort.
   - **12–15+** (curls, calves) → metabolic stress + safe near-failure for small muscles/joints.
   - Training across ranges = multiple growth mechanisms + the strength/size/work-capacity blend.

5. **Rest is prescribed, not optional.** Heavy compounds: 2.5–3 min (so you can apply force
   next set). Isolation/supersets: 60–90s (keeps density + conditioning effect up).

6. **Tempo:** unless noted, **~2s lowering, controlled lift.** Controlled eccentrics = more
   tension = more growth, and they protect joints. No bouncing, no collapsing.

## A. Barbell Back Squat — *strength + technique foundation*
**4 sets × 6–8 reps · RPE 7–8 · rest 2.5–3 min · Start: ~50 kg**

- **Why this lift / why first:** the single best driver for lower-body size and strength, and
  it's your weak point — so it gets top priority while you're freshest. Loading it directly is
  how you close the gap between upper and lower body.
- **Why 6–8 @ RPE 7–8:** heavy enough for real strength + dense muscle, but capped 2–3 reps
  shy of failure so technique stays clean (you're newer to barbell squatting) and you don't
  dig a recovery hole on 6–7h sleep.
- **Why 4 sets:** enough quality volume for a priority lift without overreaching.
- **Cues:** brace like you're about to be punched, big breath held each rep. Sit between the
  hips, knees track over toes, descend to **at least parallel** (thigh crease at knee height),
  drive the floor away. 2s down, controlled up.
- **Progression:** hit **4×8 @ RPE ≤8** → add **2.5 kg** next session, restart at 6.

## B. Romanian Deadlift (RDL) — *hamstrings + glutes (hip-hinge)*
**3 sets × 8–10 reps · RPE 8 · rest 2 min · Start: ~50 kg**

- **Why:** the squat under-trains the **hamstrings** (which act as hip extensors). The RDL is
  the best hamstring/glute hinge builder and balances the quad-dominant squat — better knee
  health + an athletic posterior chain.
- **Why 8–10 / RPE 8:** hypertrophy range; hamstrings love loaded stretch under control. RPE 8
  keeps the lower back safe on a spinal-loading lift.
- **Cues:** soft knees (not a squat), push **hips back**, bar close to legs, lower to a strong
  hamstring stretch (~mid-shin), back **flat — never round**, stand tall by squeezing glutes.
  The stretch is the point; don't chase depth by rounding.
- **Progression:** 3×10 clean → add 2.5–5 kg, restart at 8.

## C. Leg Press — *quad-focused hypertrophy (main size driver)*
**3 sets × 12–15 reps · RPE 8–9 · rest 90 sec · Start: ~100 kg total (machine-dependent)**

- **Why:** after the squat, this is where you safely **chase growth hard** — low-skill and
  stable, so you can push near failure without technique risk. Ideal for adding quad/glute
  size to lagging legs; it complements (doesn't repeat) the squat by adding volume when fatigued.
- **Why 12–15 / RPE 8–9:** higher reps + near-failure = strong stimulus, minimal joint/spinal
  stress (no bar on your back).
- **Cues:** feet shoulder-width, mid-platform. Lower controlled to ~90° knee (or just before
  the lower back rounds off the pad), press without hard lockout. Full range — no quarter reps.
- **Load note:** leg-press kg vary wildly by machine — **chase the RPE and reps, not the number.**
- **Progression:** 3×15 @ RPE ≤8 → add a plate per side, restart at 12.

## D. Superset (saves ~5 min, adds density) — *single-leg + hamstring*
Do D1 → rest 30s → D2 → rest 75–90s → repeat. **3 rounds.**

### D1. Bulgarian Split Squat (rear foot elevated) — *athleticism, glutes, balance, quad*
**3 × 10–12 per leg · RPE 8 · Start: 2 × 10 kg dumbbells**
- **Why:** your **athletic / single-leg** element. Fixes left-right imbalances (common with
  underdeveloped legs), heavily recruits glutes through a big stretch, and builds balance +
  stability that carries into sport and life. Huge bang-for-buck.
- **Cues:** weight mostly on the **front** leg, torso slightly forward to bias glutes, knee
  tracks over foot, drop straight down. Control the wobble — that's the stabilizer training.

### D2. Seated (or Lying) Leg Curl — *direct hamstring (knee-flexion)*
**3 × 12–15 · RPE 9 · Start: calibrate on machine**
- **Why:** the RDL trains hamstrings as hip-extensors; the curl trains their **other job** —
  bending the knee. Both functions = complete, injury-resistant hamstrings. It's a machine, so
  near-failure (RPE 9) is safe and effective.
- **Cues:** controlled, full squeeze at contraction, slow 2–3s release. Don't swing.

## E. Standing Calf Raise — *calves (most-neglected lower-body muscle)*
**3 sets × 12–15 · RPE 9 · rest 60 sec · Tempo: 1s pause at top, 2s stretch at bottom**
- **Why:** calves get almost nothing from squats/RDLs, so they get dedicated volume. They need
  higher reps + a deliberate **stretch + pause** to grow (built for endurance — half-reps fail).
- **Cues:** full stretch at the bottom, rise all the way onto the toes, pause and squeeze.

## F. Finisher — Conditioning (~5 min)
**Recommended: Sled Push/Drag** — 6 lengths (push down, drag back), ~20–30s work, walk back as
rest. **No sled? Bike intervals** — 6 × 20s hard / 40s easy.
- **Why the sled:** best fit for your goal blend — real conditioning + a brutal quad/glute pump,
  but **concentric-only** (no lowering phase) means **almost zero extra soreness or recovery
  cost.** Exactly right on "okay" recovery: a fat-loss/conditioning hit that won't eat into the
  growth work above. Bike is the equally-valid low-impact backup.
- **Keep it short:** 5 min. The lifting is the priority; this is a finisher, not a second workout.

| 4 | 4×6 @ 52.5 kg | New load, restart the rep climb |

Apply the **same double-progression logic to every exercise.** Always add reps first, then
load. Small repeatable jumps beat ego jumps — they compound into a much bigger squat over months.

## Autoregulation — when sleep tanks (you're at 6–7h)

Bad-sleep / high-stress day? **Don't grind:**
- Keep **A (squat)** but cut to **3 sets**, stop at RPE 7.
- Drop the **finisher**.
- Everything else: stop each set 1 rep earlier (RPE −1).

You keep the stimulus and protect recovery. Consistency over months beats heroics on a tired
Tuesday — the single biggest lever with limited sleep.

## Nutrition & recovery (the bottleneck, since you're not tracking)

Muscle = small calorie surplus + enough protein. Targets for 82 kg:
- **Protein:** ~135–165 g/day (≈1.6–2.0 g/kg). Non-negotiable for growth.
- **Calories:** grow with minimal fat at ~**maintenance + 200–300 kcal**. Proxy: if bodyweight
  is flat for 2 weeks, add ~250 kcal/day.
- **Sleep:** 6–7h is your limiter. Even **+45 min** meaningfully improves recovery → more
  volume tolerated → faster growth. Highest-ROI change available.
- **Around training:** protein + carbs before and after help performance and recovery.

## How you'll know it's working

- **Weekly:** the log shows added reps or load on most lifts.
- **2–4 weeks:** a given squat weight feels easier; you're hitting the top of rep ranges.
- **6–8 weeks:** clear strength jump on squat/RDL, visible leg development, split-squat L/R
  balance evens out.
- **Red flags:** persistent joint pain, reps dropping week over week, or unshakeable fatigue →
  a recovery/nutrition signal (sleep, food, deload), **not** a reason to add sets.

**Self-test today:** finish in ~60 min, last set of each lift lands at the prescribed RPE (not
failure on barbell lifts), and you leave with a strong quad/glute/hamstring pump but not
destroyed. Trivially easy? Your starting loads were low — bump them next session.
