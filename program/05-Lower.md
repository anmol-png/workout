# Day 5 — LOWER (Hinge / Posterior-Dominant + Unilateral)

**Target time:** ~60 min · **Working sets:** 19 + finisher
**Coverage:** glutes 7, hamstrings 6, quads 6, calves 3, core 3, conditioning

> **The second leg session — and the reason this program can fix lagging legs.** Wednesday was
> quad-dominant with a squat anchor. This is glute- and hamstring-dominant, adds the unilateral
> work, and hits the calves' other head. Together they cover the lower body completely.

## A. Barbell Hip Thrust — *the glute anchor*
**4 sets × 8–12 reps · RPE 8 · rest 2 min · Start: ~60 kg**

> **Why a hip thrust and not a deadlift.** A conventional deadlift three days after a heavy squat
> stacks spinal and central-nervous-system fatigue against a 6–7 h sleep budget, for relatively
> little hypertrophy return. The hip thrust delivers **near-maximal glute loading with almost no
> axial spinal load** — you get the growth stimulus without the recovery bill.
>
> **Want the heavier strength expression instead?** Substitute **Trap-Bar Deadlift, 3 × 5–8 @ RPE
> 7–8**. The trap bar's neutral handles and more upright torso make it far kinder to the lower
> back than a conventional pull. Both are valid; the hip thrust is the better fit for size-first.

- **Why the glutes get an anchor slot:** they're the largest muscle in the body, they're a major
  contributor to how legs look, and they're chronically underdeveloped in anyone with lagging legs.
  The hip thrust loads them at **peak contraction** — the position where they're strongest and
  where squats and RDLs give them the least tension.
- **Why 8–12 @ RPE 8:** the glutes respond well to this range with a hard squeeze at lockout.
- **Cues:** upper back on the bench at roughly the bottom of the shoulder blades. Use a bar pad.
  Chin tucked, ribs down — **do not arch the lower back to finish the rep**, that's the single most
  common error and it turns a glute exercise into a lumbar exercise. Drive through the **heels**,
  push the hips straight up to full lockout, squeeze hard for a beat, lower under control.
- **Progression:** 4×12 @ RPE ≤ 8 → +5 kg, restart at 8.
- **Substitutes:** Trap-Bar Deadlift · Glute bridge machine · Single-leg hip thrust.

## B. Bulgarian Split Squat — *unilateral: quads, glutes, balance, athleticism*
**3 sets × 8–12 reps per leg · RPE 8 · rest 90 s · Start: 2 × 10 kg dumbbells**

- **Why it earns a slot:** this is the **athleticism** element of the program, and it's the highest
  bang-for-buck single-leg exercise there is. Three distinct returns:
  1. **Fixes left–right imbalances** — very common in underdeveloped legs, and invisible on a
     barbell where the strong side quietly compensates.
  2. **Heavily recruits the glutes through a large stretch** at the bottom of each rep.
  3. **Builds balance and stabilizer strength** that carries into sport and everyday movement.
- **Why 8–12 @ RPE 8 rather than heavier:** stability is the limiter before the muscle is, and
  chasing load here just degrades the position. Moderate load, deep range, controlled.
- **Cues:** rear foot on a bench, front foot far enough forward that your **front shin stays roughly
  vertical** at the bottom. Weight mostly on the front leg — the back leg is a kickstand, not a
  contributor. Torso leaned slightly forward to bias the glutes. Drop straight down. **Control the
  wobble rather than avoiding it — the wobble is the stabilizer training.**
- **Progression:** 3×12/leg clean → next dumbbell pair, restart at 8.
- **Substitutes:** Walking Lunges · Step-ups · Reverse Lunges.

## C. Lying Leg Curl — *hamstrings, knee flexion, second dose*
**3 sets × 8–12 reps · RPE 9 · rest 90 s · Start: calibrate**

- **Why lying rather than Wednesday's seated:** the two versions differ in hip angle, and that
  changes which part of the hamstring is emphasized. Seated (hip flexed) puts the muscle under more
  stretch; lying (hip extended) shifts emphasis toward peak contraction and the short head of the
  biceps femoris. **Running both across the week covers the hamstring more completely than doing
  either twice.**
- **Why RPE 9:** machine, no risk, and hamstrings tolerate near-failure work well.
- **Cues:** hips stay down on the pad — lifting them is how you cheat this. Squeeze hard at the top,
  **slow 2–3 s negative**. The eccentric is the most valuable part of this exercise, both for growth
  and for hamstring-injury resistance.

## D. Leg Extension — *quads, isolated, second dose*
**3 sets × 12–15 reps · RPE 9 · rest 75 s · Start: calibrate**

- **Why:** the only exercise in the program that isolates the quadriceps with no hip involvement,
  and the only one that meaningfully targets the **rectus femoris** — the quad head that crosses
  the hip joint and stays partially shortened during squats and leg presses. It's a real gap that
  compounds alone don't fill.
- **Why RPE 9 and higher reps:** it's a machine with no spinal or balance demand, so it's safe to
  push hard. Higher reps also spare the knee compared with heavy low-rep extensions.
- **Cues:** full extension with a **hard 1 s squeeze at the top**, controlled negative. Don't slam
  the stack. If your knees complain, reduce the load and slow the tempo rather than dropping it.
- **Substitutes:** Hack Squat (if you'd rather have another loaded compound) · Sissy squat.

## E. Superset — *soleus calves + core*
Do **E1 → 20 s → E2 → rest 75 s → repeat. 3 rounds.**

### E1. Seated Calf Raise — *soleus*
**3 × 12–20 · RPE 9 · Start: calibrate**
- **Why seated specifically:** bending the knee takes the gastrocnemius (which crosses the knee)
  out of the movement and puts the **soleus** underneath it in charge. The soleus is a large muscle
  that adds real thickness to the lower leg, and the seated raise is essentially the only way to
  load it. Wednesday's standing raise covered the gastroc; this covers the other half.
- **Why 12–20:** the soleus is overwhelmingly slow-twitch — built for sustained low-level work. It
  responds to higher reps far better than heavy low-rep sets.
- **Cues:** full stretch at the bottom, all the way up onto the toes, pause. **No bouncing.**

### E2. Hanging Leg Raise (or Cable Crunch) — *direct core*
**3 × 10–15 · RPE 8–9 · Start: bodyweight*
- **Why direct core work at all:** every compound in this program trains the core **isometrically**
  (bracing against a load). None of them train **spinal flexion**, which is the abs' actual primary
  function. Three sets a week covers it.
- **Why hanging leg raises:** they train the abs through a long range under bodyweight load and
  build grip and shoulder stability as a bonus. **Cable crunch** is the alternative if hanging is
  awkward or your grip fails first — and it's the easier of the two to progressively load.
- **Cues:** minimize swinging — if you're using momentum, do knee raises instead until you're
  stronger. **Curl the pelvis toward the ribs** rather than just lifting the legs; hip flexors do
  the leg-lifting, the abs do the pelvic tilt. Control the descent.

## F. Finisher — Conditioning (~5 min)
**Sled Drag/Push** — 6 lengths, ~20–30 s of work each, walk back as rest.
**No sled? Bike intervals** — 6 × 20 s hard / 40 s easy.

- **Why it's concentric-only:** no lowering phase means little muscle damage and minimal added
  soreness, so you get genuine conditioning without paying for it in Monday's recovery. On a 6–7 h
  sleep budget that trade-off is the whole reason this finisher is a sled and not a run.
- **A backward sled drag** biases the quads and is easy on the knees — a good variation to alternate
  with Wednesday's forward push.
- **5 minutes. Not more.** The lifting is the priority.
