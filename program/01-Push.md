# Day 1 — PUSH (Chest · Shoulders · Triceps)

**Target time:** ~60 min · **Working sets:** 18 · **Coverage:** chest 7, delts 6, triceps 5

> Read [00-Program-Overview.md](00-Program-Overview.md) first — RPE, double progression and
> autoregulation are defined there and apply to every exercise below.

---

## Warm-up (~7 min — part of the session)

| # | What | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 3 min easy bike or rower | Core temperature and blood flow; joints move better warm |
| 2 | Band pull-aparts × 20 | Wakes up rear delts and upper back — the muscles that keep your shoulders in a safe position under a bar |
| 3 | Shoulder dislocates / pass-throughs × 10 | Opens the shoulder girdle for pressing and overhead work |
| 4 | Scap push-ups × 10 | Teaches the shoulder blades to move and set properly |
| 5 | Bench ramp: bar × 10 → 30 kg × 5 → 40 kg × 3 | Primes the nervous system and rehearses the groove. **Not working sets** — no fatigue. |

## A. Barbell Bench Press — *the pressing strength anchor*
**4 sets × 5–8 reps · RPE 7–8 · rest 2.5–3 min · Start: ~45–50 kg**

- **Why first:** heaviest, most technical, most fatiguing lift of the day. It gets your freshest
  nervous system. It's also the session's strength expression — the one place you're training for
  force production, not just size.
- **Why 5–8 @ RPE 7–8:** the lower end of the hypertrophy range gives you dense muscle *and*
  strength carryover. RPE 7–8 keeps you 2–3 reps shy of failure, which matters enormously on
  bench — a failed rep with a loaded bar over your chest is the one genuinely dangerous outcome in
  this program.
- **Why 4 sets:** enough volume to drive the main chest stimulus without eating the session.
- **Cues:** shoulder blades pulled back and **down**, pinned to the bench — this creates a stable
  base and protects the shoulder joint. Feet planted, slight arch in the upper back. Bar touches
  around the lower chest/sternum, elbows at ~45–60° from the torso (not flared to 90°). 2 s down,
  drive up. Keep the bar path slightly diagonal, back toward the shoulders.
- **Safety:** use safety pins in the rack or a spotter. Always.
- **Progression:** 4×8 @ RPE ≤ 8 → **+2.5 kg**, restart at 5.
- **Substitutes:** Dumbbell Bench Press (greater range of motion, easier on shoulders, no spotter
  needed) · Machine Chest Press.

## B. Incline Dumbbell Press (30°) — *upper chest*
**3 sets × 8–12 reps · RPE 8 · rest 2 min · Start: 2 × 16 kg**

- **Why:** the flat bench under-trains the **clavicular (upper) head** of the chest. Upper chest is
  also the region that most visibly changes how a physique reads. A 30° incline biases it without
  turning the lift into a shoulder press — go steeper than ~45° and the front delt takes over.
- **Why dumbbells:** each arm works independently, so your stronger side can't compensate for the
  weaker one. They also allow a deeper stretch at the bottom than a bar, and loaded stretch is a
  potent growth stimulus.
- **Why 8–12 @ RPE 8:** prime hypertrophy range, and dumbbells are safe to push here — worst case
  you set them down.
- **Cues:** control the descent to a real stretch across the chest, don't clang the dumbbells
  together at the top (that unloads the chest), keep tension throughout. Elbows tucked ~45°.
- **Progression:** 3×12 clean → next dumbbell pair up, restart at 8.
- **Substitutes:** Incline Barbell Press · Incline Machine Press.

## C. Seated Dumbbell Shoulder Press — *front and side delts*
**3 sets × 8–12 reps · RPE 8 · rest 90 s · Start: 2 × 12 kg**

- **Why:** direct vertical pressing. The bench works front delts as a secondary, but the delts need
  a movement where they're the prime mover to grow properly. Seated (back supported) removes lower
  back and balance as limiting factors, so the shoulders get the stimulus rather than your torso.
- **Why after the chest work:** it shares the triceps and front delts with pressing, and it's the
  less important of the two for your goals. Chest gets the fresh slots.
- **Cues:** press slightly in front of the head, not behind. Lower until the elbows are roughly at
  shoulder height — going deeper adds shoulder strain, not growth. Ribs down, don't arch the lower
  back to complete the rep.
- **Progression:** 3×12 → next pair up, restart at 8.
- **Substitutes:** Machine Shoulder Press · Standing Barbell Overhead Press (more athletic, more
  core demand, less isolated).

## D. Superset — *side delts + triceps*
Do **D1 → 20 s → D2 → rest 75–90 s → repeat. 3 rounds.** Saves ~5 min and raises session density.
They share nothing, so neither compromises the other.

### D1. Cable Lateral Raise — *side delts (shoulder width)*
**3 × 12–20 · RPE 9 · Start: calibrate (light — this is a small muscle)**
- **Why cable, not dumbbell:** a dumbbell lateral raise has almost no resistance at the bottom
  (gravity pulls straight down, the arm is vertical) and peaks at the top. A cable set behind you
  keeps **tension across the whole range**, including the stretched position — meaningfully more
  effective per set.
- **Why 12–20 and RPE 9:** side delts are small, endurance-biased, and low-risk. High reps close
  to failure is where they respond. There is no injury cost to pushing hard here.
- **Cues:** lead with the elbow, raise to about shoulder height, no higher. Minimal swing — if
  you're using body English, the weight is wrong. Slow negative.
- **Note:** side delt width is one of the highest-visual-return muscles to develop. Do not skip it.

### D2. Overhead Rope Triceps Extension — *long head of the triceps*
**3 × 10–15 · RPE 9 · Start: calibrate**
- **Why overhead specifically:** the triceps' **long head** crosses the shoulder joint, so it's
  only fully stretched when your arm is overhead. Pushdowns barely train it. The long head is the
  largest of the three heads and the one that gives the arm its mass from the back.
- **Cues:** elbows stay pointed forward and relatively fixed — the upper arm shouldn't drift. Get a
  deep stretch behind the head, extend to a full lockout, squeeze.

## E. Cable Triceps Pushdown — *lateral and medial heads, finisher*
**2 sets × 12–15 · RPE 9–10 · rest 60 s · Start: calibrate**
- **Why last and why only 2 sets:** the triceps have already done four sets of heavy pressing plus
  three overhead sets. This is a targeted top-up on the heads the overhead extension emphasizes
  less, at the point in the session where nothing else depends on fresh triceps.
- **Why RPE 9–10 is fine here:** a failed pushdown means the cable stack goes down. Zero risk, so
  take the last set to true failure.
- **Cues:** upper arms pinned to your sides, full lockout, controlled return. Don't lean over the
  weight and turn it into a press.
- **Substitutes:** Bench Dips · Single-arm cable pushdown.
