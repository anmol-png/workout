# Program Overview — 5-Day PPLUL

> **Built for:** 26M · 82 kg · fully equipped gym · intermediate upper body, **lagging legs** ·
> 6–7 h sleep · goal = **size first**, blended with strength, athleticism and conditioning.
> **Status: fresh start.** Week 1 is calibration, not progression.

---

## 1. The split

| Day | Session | Emphasis | Finisher |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mon** | [Push](01-Push.md) | Chest, delts, triceps | — |
| **Tue** | [Pull](02-Pull.md) | Back, rear delts, biceps | — |
| **Wed** | [Legs](03-Legs.md) | Quad-dominant, squat anchor | Sled / bike, 5 min |
| **Thu** | Rest | Walk, 8–10k steps | — |
| **Fri** | [Upper](04-Upper.md) | Second chest/back/delt/arm dose | — |
| **Sat** | [Lower](05-Lower.md) | Hinge/posterior + unilateral | Sled / bike, 5 min |
| **Sun** | Rest | Walk, 8–10k steps | — |

### Why this exact ordering

- **Every muscle is trained 2×/week.** Once weekly volume is equated, twice-weekly frequency
  matches or beats once-weekly for hypertrophy — and it lets you spread hard sets across two
  sessions instead of grinding 20 sets for one muscle in a single fatiguing block.
- **Legs land Wed and Sat** — 3 days apart, then 4. Neither leg session is ever run on the
  residual fatigue of the other. This matters because legs are the priority.
- **Thursday's rest sits between Legs and Upper**, the two most demanding days.
- **Push before Pull.** If Pull came first, fatigued biceps wouldn't matter much — but if Pull
  came first you'd also be fresh for nothing in particular. Push-first means triceps aren't
  pre-fatigued when you bench, which is the session's heaviest lift.
- **Upper (Fri) and Lower (Sat) back-to-back is fine** — they share no muscle groups.

## 2. Weekly volume — the number that actually matters

Weekly **hard sets per muscle** is the primary driver of hypertrophy. Frequency and exercise
selection mostly matter as ways to *distribute* that volume well. The productive band is roughly
**10–20 hard sets per muscle per week**; below 10 you're under-stimulating, above ~20 the return
per set collapses and the recovery cost does not.

A set counts **1.0** toward each muscle it works directly and **0.5** toward each muscle it works
as an assistant — a bench press is a chest set and half a triceps set. That's closer to the truth
than counting it fully for both (which wildly inflates arms) or ignoring the assistance entirely.
These are the exact numbers the tracker computes:

| Muscle | Sets/wk | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **Quads** | **13** | Legs 7 · Lower 6 |
| **Hamstrings** | **13** | Legs 8 · Lower 5 |
| **Glutes** | **12** | Legs 5 · Lower 7 |
| Calves | 6 | Legs 3 · Lower 3 |
| Chest | 13 | Push 7 · Upper 6 |
| Back | 17.5 | Pull 10 · Upper 6 · RDL 1.5 |
| Side delts | 9 | Push 6 · Upper 3 |
| Rear delts | 7.5 | Pull 3 direct + row spillover |
| Front delts | 9.5 | All pressing |
| Biceps | 15 | Pull 5 · Upper 2 direct + all pulling |
| Triceps | 16.5 | Push 5 · Upper 5 direct + all pressing |
| Core | 6.5 | Lower 3 direct + compound bracing |

**90 working sets/week, 35 of them on the two leg days — 39 % of everything you do.** That
allocation is the entire point of the program: it's what makes this a leg-lag correction rather
than a generic bodybuilding split. The tracker charts this weekly so you can *verify* it's
happening rather than assume it.

> **Arms get only 7 direct sets between them on Upper day, deliberately.** They accumulate to 15–16
> counted sets anyway through spillover from every press, row and pulldown. Giving them a third
> direct set each would cost recovery that legs have a better claim on.

> Everything else sits mid-band deliberately. On 6–7 h sleep, the ceiling on productive volume is
> lower than the textbook maximum. Pushing chest to 20 sets would cost leg recovery — that trade
> is backwards for your goal.

## 3. The progression engine — double progression

Every exercise has a **rep range**. Start at the bottom.

> When you hit the **top of the rep range on all working sets at or below the target RPE**,
> add the smallest increment next session and drop back to the bottom of the range.

**Add reps first. Add load second. Always.**

Increments: **2.5 kg** on barbells · **next dumbbell pair** (usually +2 kg/hand) · **one notch**
on machines · **one plate per side** on leg press.

**Worked example — Back Squat, 4 × 5–8 @ RPE 7–8:**

| Week | Session | What happened |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 4×5 @ 50 kg | Calibration. Confirm it lands at RPE 7. |
| 2 | 4×6 @ 50 kg | Added a rep to every set. |
| 3 | 4×7 @ 50 kg | Added another. |
| 4 | 4×8 @ 50 kg | **Top of range, all sets, RPE ≤ 8** → load goes up. |
| 5 | 4×5 @ 52.5 kg | New load, restart the rep climb. |

That's +2.5 kg/month on squat from a 50 kg start — which compounds to a meaningfully different
squat inside a year. Small repeatable jumps beat ego jumps every time.

**Stalling:** three sessions with no added reps or load on a lift → take a 10 % back-off on that
lift for one session, or rotate to its listed substitute. A stall is information, not failure.

## 4. RPE — how hard each set should be

RPE = **reps left in the tank.**

| RPE | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 10 | Failure — 0 reps left |
| 9 | 1 rep left |
| 8 | 2 reps left |
| 7 | 3 reps left |

**We train RPE 7–9.** Hard enough to grow, short of the grinding failure that wrecks recovery —
which matters more than usual at 6–7 h sleep.

- **Barbell compounds → RPE 7–8.** Stay 2–3 reps shy. Protects technique and joints, and taking
  a heavy squat or bench to failure costs far more recovery than the extra rep is worth.
- **Machines and isolation → RPE 9.** Low skill, stable, low joint stress. Safe to push close to
  failure, and that's where these exercises do their best work.

Training close to but not at failure captures nearly all the hypertrophy of failure training at a
fraction of the fatigue cost. On your sleep budget that trade is strongly in your favour.

## 5. Mesocycle — a 6-week block

| Week | Instruction |
|---|---|
| **1** | **Calibration.** Bottom of every rep range. Compounds capped at RPE 7. Find your real loads and write them down. **Do not chase progression.** |
| **2–5** | **Accumulation.** Double progression on every lift. If recovery is holding, add 1 set to Legs and Lower in weeks 3 and 5. |
| **6** | **Deload.** Half the sets, same loads, RPE ≤ 6. |

**Week 1 is not wasted.** Starting too heavy is the single most common way an intermediate program
dies in week 3 — you burn your recovery on loads you haven't earned, form degrades, and the
progression has nowhere to go. Starting light gives you four clean weeks of climbing.

**The deload is not optional.** Adaptation is realized during recovery, not during work. Skipping
week 6 doesn't get you 6 weeks of gains — it gets you a week 7 that feels like week 6 and a week 8
where the numbers go backwards.

After week 6: re-test, then start Block 2 with the new loads and rotated accessory variations
(swap the listed substitutes in). Compounds stay; accessories rotate.

## 6. Autoregulation — mandatory, not optional

You sleep 6–7 h. That is the binding constraint on this entire program, and some weeks will be
worse. **Grinding through a bad day is how you end up injured or stalled.**

**Trigger:** sleep < 6 h, or you rate readiness ≤ 2 out of 5.

**The cut:**
1. Drop the **last set** of every exercise.
2. Subtract **1 from every RPE target** (stop one rep earlier everywhere).
3. **Skip the finisher.**

You keep ~80 % of the stimulus for ~60 % of the fatigue. The app detects this from your sleep
entry and offers the adjusted session automatically.

**Consistency over months beats heroics on a tired Tuesday.** This is the highest-leverage rule
in the document.

## 7. Universals (apply to every session)

- **Warm-up: ~6–8 min.** 3–4 min easy bike, dynamic mobility for the day's pattern, then 2–3
  ramping sets on the first exercise. Ramp sets are **not** working sets — they prime the nervous
  system without adding fatigue.
- **Tempo:** ~2 s lowering, controlled lift, no bouncing. The eccentric is where a large share of
  the growth stimulus lives, and controlling it protects joints.
- **Full range of motion**, especially the stretched position. Partial reps at the top are the
  cheapest way to make a set feel hard while doing less.
- **Rest as prescribed.** 2.5–3 min on heavy compounds so you can actually apply force on the next
  set; 60–90 s on isolation. Cutting compound rest to save time costs you reps, which costs you
  the progression.
- **Log every working set.** No log → no precise overload → plateau. That's what the app is for.

## 8. How you'll know it's working

| Horizon | Signal |
|---|---|
| **Weekly** | The log shows added reps or load on most lifts. |
| **2–4 weeks** | A given load feels easier; you're reaching the top of rep ranges. |
| **6–8 weeks** | Clear strength jump on squat / bench / hip thrust. Visible leg development. Bulgarian split squat L/R balance evens out. |
| **12 weeks** | Bodyweight up ~2.5–4 kg at 0.2–0.4 kg/week, mostly lean. Legs no longer the obvious weak point. |

**Red flags** — persistent joint pain, reps dropping week over week, or fatigue that a rest day
doesn't touch. These mean **recovery or nutrition**, not insufficient volume. The correct response
is more sleep, more food, or a deload — never more sets.
