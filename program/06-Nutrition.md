# Nutrition & Recovery

> **This is the bottleneck.** The training plan is already good enough. If results don't show in
> 8–12 weeks, it will almost certainly be because of what's on this page — not because the program
> needed more sets.

**Built for:** 82 kg, 26M, 5 training days/week, goal = **lean gain** (add muscle, minimize fat).

---

## 1. Your daily targets

| Target | Number | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| **Calories** | **~2,900 kcal/day** | Maintenance ≈ 33 kcal/kg ≈ **2,700**, plus a **~250 kcal surplus** |
| **Protein** | **165 g/day** | 2.0 g/kg — the top of the useful 1.6–2.2 g/kg range |
| **Fat** | **75 g/day** | 0.9 g/kg — enough for hormonal health, leaves room for carbs |
| **Carbs** | **~390 g/day** | The remainder. Fuels five sessions a week. |
| **Water** | 3–3.5 L/day | Performance drops measurably when you're even mildly dehydrated |

**Why a *small* surplus.** Muscle gain has a hard biological speed limit. Eating +250 kcal and
eating +800 kcal build muscle at roughly the same rate — the difference is almost entirely fat.
A small surplus means you can run this for six months without needing a cut afterwards.

### The feedback loop that makes the calorie number self-correcting

You will not estimate your maintenance perfectly. You don't have to — **let the scale tell you.**

> Weigh yourself first thing in the morning, after the bathroom, before eating. **Every day.**
> Only ever look at the **7-day average** — daily weight swings 1–2 kg on water, salt and food
> volume and means nothing on its own.

| 7-day average, over 2 weeks | Do this |
|---|---|
| Gaining **0.2–0.4 kg/week** | Correct. Change nothing. |
| **Flat** | Add ~250 kcal/day |
| Gaining **> 0.5 kg/week** | Subtract ~200 kcal/day — you're adding fat faster than muscle |
| **Losing** | Add ~350 kcal/day |

**Target rate: 0.2–0.4 kg/week** (≈ 0.25–0.5 % of bodyweight). Over 12 weeks that's ~2.5–4 kg,
mostly lean. The app tracks this automatically and tells you when to adjust.

## 2. Protein — the part that actually fails

**165 g/day, split across ~4 meals of ~40 g.** The reason to spread it out rather than eat it all
at dinner: each feeding stimulates muscle protein synthesis for a few hours, and ~35–45 g of
quality protein is roughly where that response saturates for someone your size. Four hits beat one.

**Hitting 165 g without planning it is essentially impossible on a normal Indian diet** — which is
carb-dense and protein-light by default. This is the single most common failure point. Plan it.

### Protein content of common Indian foods

| Food | Serving | Protein |
|---|---|---|
| **Soya chunks** (dry weight) | 50 g | **~26 g** ← highest per rupee, by a wide margin |
| **Whey protein** | 1 scoop (30 g) | ~24 g |
| **Chicken breast** | 100 g cooked | ~31 g |
| **Paneer** | 100 g | ~18 g (but ~20 g fat — counts against your fat budget) |
| **Fish** (rohu, surmai, basa) | 100 g | ~20 g |
| **Eggs** | 2 whole | ~12 g |
| **Egg whites** | 4 | ~14 g |
| **Greek yogurt / hung curd** | 150 g | ~15 g |
| **Milk** (full fat) | 250 ml | ~8 g |
| **Toor / moong dal** (cooked) | 1 katori (150 g) | ~9 g |
| **Rajma / chana** (cooked) | 1 katori | ~9 g |
| **Tofu** | 100 g | ~11 g |
| **Regular curd** | 150 g | ~5 g |
| **Roti** (wheat) | 1 medium | ~3 g |
| **Rice** (cooked) | 1 katori | ~3 g |

> **Note on dal and rajma:** these are good foods, but a katori of dal has about 9 g of protein and
> ~150 kcal. Reaching 165 g on dal alone would take eighteen katoris. Treat pulses as a
> contribution, not a strategy — the strategy is soya, whey, eggs, chicken/fish, curd and paneer.

> **Dal + rice together** form a complete amino acid profile (pulses are low in methionine, rice is
> low in lysine; each covers the other's gap). Standard Indian pairing, and it works.

### A sample 165 g day

| Meal | Food | Protein |
|---|---|---|
| **Breakfast** | 3 whole eggs + 3 egg whites, 2 rotis, milk (250 ml) | ~40 g |
| **Lunch** | 150 g chicken or 100 g paneer, dal, rice, sabzi, curd | ~45 g |
| **Pre/post workout** | 1 whey scoop + a banana | ~25 g |
| **Dinner** | 50 g soya chunks in curry, 2 rotis, sabzi, 150 g curd | ~40 g |
| **Before bed** | 250 ml milk or 100 g Greek yogurt | ~10–15 g |
| | | **≈ 165 g** |

**Vegetarian?** Soya chunks (26 g per 50 g dry), paneer, curd, whey and dal will get you there —
it needs more deliberate planning but it's entirely doable.

## 3. Around training

> **See [07-Pre-Workout.md](07-Pre-Workout.md) for the full protocol** — the 4pm fuelling meal,
> caffeine timing maths, and the supplements worth taking. Summary below.

- **~90 min before:** 40–60 g carbs + 20–25 g protein, **low fat and low fibre** (both slow gastric
  emptying and leave the meal sitting in your stomach while you squat). For a 5:30pm session that
  means a **4pm meal** — without it you're training on a five-hour fast.
- **After:** protein + carbs within a couple of hours. The "30-minute anabolic window" is a myth —
  what matters is total daily protein and not going many hours without any. Convenience is the real
  argument for a post-workout shake.
- **Caffeine: with brunch, ≤200 mg, hard cutoff 1pm.** Its half-life is ~5–6 h (8+ h if you're a slow
  metaboliser), so a 1pm coffee is *still* delivering ~113 mg at a 5:30pm session while leaving only
  ~47 mg at bedtime — versus ~78–104 mg from a 5pm dose. On a 6–7 h sleep budget that difference is
  the whole ballgame.

## 4. Supplements — only the ones with real evidence

| Supplement | Dose | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Creatine monohydrate** | **5 g/day**, any time, every day including rest days | **Take it.** The most thoroughly evidenced legal performance supplement that exists. Improves strength, work capacity and lean mass. No loading phase needed. Cheap. Monohydrate only — the fancier forms cost more and do nothing extra. |
| **Whey protein** | As needed | A convenience food, not a magic one. Useful precisely because hitting 165 g from whole food alone is hard. |
| **Vitamin D3** | 1000–2000 IU/day | Worth it if you're indoors most days — deficiency is very common in India despite the sunshine, and it affects strength and mood. Get a blood test if you want certainty. |
| **Caffeine** | 3–6 mg/kg pre-workout | Works. Respect the timing rule above. |
| **Everything else** | — | BCAAs, glutamine, testosterone boosters, mass gainers, pre-workout blends: skip them. Money better spent on food. |

## 5. Sleep — your actual limiting factor

You sleep **6–7 hours**. Everything in this program has been scaled down to accommodate that:
volume is mid-band, compounds are capped at RPE 7–8, and autoregulation is a mandatory rule rather
than a suggestion.

> **Adding 45 minutes of sleep is the highest-return change available to you — larger than any
> supplement, any training tweak, and probably any nutrition adjustment.**

Sleep is when growth hormone is released, when muscle protein synthesis peaks, and when your
nervous system recovers enough to produce force again. Under-sleeping doesn't just slow recovery;
it measurably reduces the volume of training you can productively absorb. At 7.5–8 h, this exact
program would produce noticeably better results with no other change.

**What actually moves the needle:**
- A fixed wake time, seven days a week — it anchors the whole rhythm more effectively than a fixed
  bedtime.
- No caffeine after ~2 pm.
- Cool, fully dark room.
- Screens down 30 min before bed.
- **Log your sleep in the app.** It's what triggers the autoregulation prompt on bad days, and
  seeing the number correlate with your session quality is unusually motivating.

## 6. The whole thing in eight lines

1. **~2,900 kcal/day.** Adjust by 250 based on the 7-day weight average, every 2 weeks.
2. **165 g protein/day**, spread across ~4 meals. This is the one that fails — plan it.
3. **75 g fat, ~390 g carbs.** Carbs fuel five sessions a week; don't fear them.
4. **Weigh daily, judge weekly.** Target 0.2–0.4 kg/week gain.
5. **5 g creatine monohydrate, daily.** Take it.
6. **3–3.5 L water.**
7. **Sleep is the bottleneck.** +45 min beats every supplement combined.
8. **Consistency beats optimization.** 85 % adherence for six months crushes 100 % for three weeks.
