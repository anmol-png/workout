# Day 3 — LEGS (Quad-Dominant · Squat Anchor)

**Target time:** ~60 min · **Working sets:** 16 + finisher
**Coverage:** quads 7, hamstrings 6, glutes ~5, calves 3, conditioning

> **This is the priority session of the week.** Legs are your lagging body part, and this is the
> day the squat gets loaded while you're fresh. If a week goes badly and you have to drop a
> session, this is not the one you drop.

> Read [00-Program-Overview.md](00-Program-Overview.md) first — RPE, double progression and
> autoregulation are defined there.

---

## Warm-up (~8 min — genuinely do not skip this one)

| # | What | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 4 min easy bike or incline walk | Raises core temperature; cold hips and knees squat badly |
| 2 | Leg swings × 10/leg, front-back and side-side | Opens the hips for depth |
| 3 | Bodyweight squats × 12, slow, to full depth | Grooves the pattern, mobilizes ankles and hips |
| 4 | Glute bridges × 15 | **Activates the glutes so they actually fire during the squat.** Critical for underdeveloped legs — dormant glutes let the quads and lower back take over. |
| 5 | Squat ramp: bar × 8 → 30 kg × 5 → 40 kg × 3 | Nervous system priming and technique rehearsal. **Not working sets.** |

## A. Barbell Back Squat — *the single most important lift in this program*
**4 sets × 5–8 reps · RPE 7–8 · rest 3 min · Start: ~45–50 kg**

- **Why it leads everything:** the back squat is the best available driver of lower-body size and
  strength, and it is precisely your weak point. It gets the freshest nervous system of the week.
  Loading it directly and consistently is how the gap between your upper and lower body closes.
- **Why 5–8 @ RPE 7–8:** heavy enough to build real strength and dense muscle, capped 2–3 reps shy
  of failure. That cap does two things — it keeps technique clean while you're still building the
  barbell squat pattern, and it stops you digging a recovery hole you can't climb out of on 6–7 h
  sleep. A squat set taken to failure costs several days of readiness.
- **Why 3 minutes of rest:** anything less and your second, third and fourth sets are limited by
  fatigue rather than strength. You'd be doing the same work with less load — worse stimulus, same
  time. Rest is part of the prescription.
- **Cues:**
  - Brace as though you're about to be punched in the stomach. Big breath, hold it for the rep.
  - Bar on the upper traps/rear delts, hands as narrow as your shoulders allow comfortably.
  - Sit **between** the hips, not just back. Knees track over the toes and are allowed to travel
    forward — that's normal and safe.
  - Descend to **at least parallel** (hip crease level with the top of the knee). Depth is
    non-negotiable; a half squat trains a fraction of the muscle.
  - Drive the floor away. Chest stays up, hips and shoulders rise together.
- **Progression:** 4×8 @ RPE ≤ 8 → **+2.5 kg**, restart at 5.
- **Substitutes:** Front Squat (more quad, less lower back, demands more mobility) · Hack Squat ·
  Safety Bar Squat.

## B. Romanian Deadlift — *hamstrings and glutes as hip extensors*
**3 sets × 8–10 reps · RPE 8 · rest 2 min · Start: ~50 kg**

- **Why it's on the quad day:** the squat substantially under-trains the hamstrings. Pairing a
  hinge with the squat in the same session means every leg day covers both sides of the thigh —
  better knee health, a more athletic posterior chain, and no muscle waiting a full week for
  attention.
- **Why 8–10 @ RPE 8:** hamstrings respond exceptionally well to **loaded stretch under control**,
  which is exactly what the RDL's lowering phase is. RPE 8 keeps the lower back out of trouble on
  a lift that loads the spine in a hinged position.
- **Cues:**
  - **Soft knees, not bent.** This is a hinge, not a squat. The knee angle stays roughly constant.
  - Push the **hips backward** — that's the movement. The bar lowering is a consequence.
  - Bar stays in contact with or very close to your legs the whole way down.
  - Lower until you feel a strong hamstring stretch (usually mid-shin). **Back flat — never
    rounded.** Do not chase depth by rounding; the stretch is the goal, not the floor.
  - Stand up by squeezing the glutes.
- **Progression:** 3×10 clean → +2.5–5 kg, restart at 8.
- **Substitutes:** Dumbbell RDL · Good Morning · 45° Back Extension.

## C. Leg Press — *the main quad size driver*
**3 sets × 10–15 reps · RPE 8–9 · rest 90 s · Start: ~100 kg total (machine-dependent)**

- **Why:** this is where you **chase growth hard**. It's low-skill, fully stable and supported,
  which means you can push genuinely close to failure without technique risk or spinal loading —
  something you cannot safely do on a squat. For adding size to lagging quads, that's exactly the
  tool you want.
- **Why it doesn't just repeat the squat:** it adds high-quality quad volume at a point in the
  session where you're already fatigued and a barbell on your back would be a bad idea. Different
  role, not a duplicate.
- **Why 10–15 @ RPE 8–9:** higher reps and near-failure produce a strong stimulus with minimal
  joint and spinal stress.
- **Cues:** feet about shoulder-width, mid-platform. Lower under control to roughly 90° at the
  knee — or stop just before your lower back rounds off the pad, whichever comes first (rounding
  there is the main injury risk on this machine). Press without slamming into lockout. **Full
  range — quarter reps with more plates is the most common way to waste this exercise.**
- **Load note:** leg press weights vary wildly between machines. **Chase the reps and the RPE, not
  the number on the stack.**
- **Progression:** 3×15 @ RPE ≤ 8 → add a plate per side, restart at 10.
- **Substitutes:** Hack Squat · Pendulum Squat · Walking Lunges.

## D. Superset — *hamstring curl + calves*
Do **D1 → 20 s → D2 → rest 75 s → repeat. 3 rounds.**

### D1. Seated Leg Curl — *hamstrings as knee flexors*
**3 × 10–15 · RPE 9 · Start: calibrate**
- **Why, when you already did RDLs:** the hamstrings have **two jobs** — extending the hip (RDL)
  and bending the knee (curl). Training only one leaves the muscle partly developed and is a
  well-known contributor to hamstring strains. Both functions, every week.
- **Why *seated* rather than lying:** the seated version keeps the hip flexed, which puts the
  hamstrings under greater stretch during the movement. Evidence favours it for growth.
- **Why RPE 9:** it's a machine. Failure here is completely safe and the muscle benefits.
- **Cues:** controlled throughout, squeeze hard at peak contraction, **slow 2–3 s release**. Don't
  swing the weight or let your hips lift off the seat.

### D2. Standing Calf Raise — *gastrocnemius*
**3 × 10–15 · RPE 9 · Tempo: 2 s stretch at the bottom, 1 s pause at the top · Start: calibrate**
- **Why dedicated work:** calves get almost nothing from squats and RDLs. They're built for
  repeated submaximal contraction all day, so they need deliberate loading through a **full range
  with a real stretch** to grow. Bouncing half-reps — the default in most gyms — do essentially
  nothing.
- **Why standing:** a straight knee lengthens the gastrocnemius, which is the head that gives the
  calf its shape. (The seated version on Lower day covers the soleus underneath.)
- **Cues:** full stretch at the bottom — let the heel drop as far as the platform allows and hold
  it. Rise all the way onto the toes and pause. The tempo *is* the exercise here.

## F. Finisher — Conditioning (~5 min)
**Sled Push/Drag** — 6 lengths, push down and drag back, ~20–30 s of work each, walk back as rest.
**No sled? Bike intervals** — 6 × 20 s hard / 40 s easy.

- **Why the sled is the best fit:** it delivers real conditioning and a brutal quad/glute pump, but
  it's **concentric-only** — there's no lowering phase, which is where most muscle damage and
  soreness come from. That means **almost zero additional recovery cost.** On a 6–7 h sleep budget
  that's exactly the trade you want: a genuine cardiovascular and fat-loss stimulus that doesn't
  eat into the growth work above it.
- **Keep it to 5 minutes.** This is a finisher, not a second workout. The lifting is the priority.
