# Pre-Workout Energy — Fuel, Not Stimulants

> **Your situation:** train **5:30–6:30pm** · asleep **midnight–1am** · currently eat brunch, then
> a coffee, then go. Pre-workout supplements gave you energy but wrecked your sleep.
>
> **The diagnosis:** you don't have a stimulant problem. You have a **five-hour fasting gap**
> before the hardest hour of your day, and you were using caffeine to paper over it.

---

## 1. "Energy" is three separate mechanisms

Pre-workout powders deliberately blur these together. Separating them is most of the solution.

| What you feel | What's actually happening | What controls it | Sleep cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Lasting 60 min without fading** | Muscle glycogen + blood glucose | **Food.** Nothing substitutes for it. | none |
| **Power set to set** | Phosphocreatine regenerating ATP | **Creatine** | none |
| **Late-set endurance, the pump** | Blood flow, ammonia + H⁺ buffering | Citrulline, beta-alanine | none |
| **"Switched on," drive, focus** | Adenosine blocked at the receptor | **Caffeine** | **high** |

**Caffeine does not create energy.** It blocks the receptor that tells your brain you're tired.
The fatigue accumulates anyway — you just stop perceiving it, and then pay it back that night.
On a 6–7 h sleep budget that is the worst available trade, because sleep is already the binding
constraint on everything in this program.

The first three rows are what actually carry a 60-minute lifting session. **None of them touch
your sleep.**

---

## 2. The actual problem: a five-hour hole

Brunch around midday → training at 5:30pm is a **4½–5½ hour gap**. By the time you unrack the
first bar, blood glucose is low, liver glycogen is drawn down, and you're asking for 16–19 hard
sets on fumes. That flat feeling isn't a missing supplement. It's an empty tank.

**A stimulant on an empty tank makes it worse**, not better: you feel able to push, the fuel to
actually push isn't there, and the session degrades in the back half — exactly where your leg
volume lives.

### The fix: one meal at 4pm

| Time | What | Why |
|---|---|---|
| ~12–1pm | **Brunch** (as now) + **coffee — see §4** | Main meal; the caffeine goes here, not later |
| **~4:00pm** | **The pre-workout meal — this is the change** | 40–60 g carbs, 20–25 g protein, **low fat, low fibre** |
| ~5:15pm | *Optional* fast carbs if you feel flat: banana or 4 dates | ~25 g quick glucose |
| 5:30–6:30pm | **Train.** Water throughout. | |
| ~7:15pm | Dinner: protein + carbs | Recovery + you've a protein target to hit |

**Why 90 minutes out, and why low fat and low fibre:** fat and fibre both slow gastric emptying.
Eat paneer or a heavy oily meal at 4:30pm and it's still sitting in your stomach during squats —
that's the sluggish, heavy feeling people misdiagnose as "needing a stronger pre-workout."

### What to actually eat at 4pm — pick one

| Option | Carbs | Protein |
|---|---|---|
| 1 bowl **poha** + 150 g curd | ~50 g | ~20 g |
| 2 slices white toast + honey + **1 scoop whey** | ~45 g | ~26 g |
| **Banana + 4–5 dates + 1 scoop whey** *(easiest)* | ~55 g | ~25 g |
| **Upma** + banana | ~55 g | ~12 g |
| Small katori **white rice + dal** + curd | ~50 g | ~16 g |
| **Sweet potato** (200 g) + whey | ~45 g | ~25 g |

> **White rice and white bread are correct here** — this is the one time of day where *fast*
> digestion beats "healthy and slow." Save the brown rice and oats for other meals.

> **This meal solves two problems at once.** You're eating roughly 2–3 times a day, which makes
> 165 g of protein very hard to reach. A 4pm feed with 25 g of protein in it is both your
> pre-workout fuel *and* the fourth protein feeding you were missing.

### Intra-workout — only on Legs and Lower days

For a 60-minute session you don't need carbs mid-workout. The two exceptions are your **hardest**
days: sip **25–30 g of carbs** through Legs (Wed) and Lower (Sat) — 300 ml fruit juice, coconut
water with honey, or a scoop of dextrose in your bottle. It measurably holds up output in the back
half of the session, which is where the leg volume that matters most sits.

## 3. Supplements that actually work, ranked by evidence

### ⭐ Creatine monohydrate — 5 g/day, any time
**The single best "energy" supplement for lifting, and it isn't close.** It expands your
phosphocreatine stores, which is the system regenerating ATP during the first 10 seconds of every
set — precisely the energy system a heavy set of 5–8 uses.

- **Evidence: unambiguous.** Hundreds of trials. More reps, more strength, more lean mass.
- **Timing is irrelevant** — it works by saturating muscle over weeks, not by an acute hit.
  Take it whenever you'll remember. Rest days too.
- **Monohydrate only.** HCl, buffered, "advanced" forms cost more and do nothing extra.
- No loading phase needed; 5 g/day saturates in 3–4 weeks.
- **Zero effect on sleep.**

### ⭐ Citrulline malate — 8 g, 60 minutes pre-workout
**This is your replacement for the pre-workout scoop.** It's what actually produces the pump and
the extra reps at the end of a set, minus the stimulation.

- Raises plasma arginine → nitric oxide → blood flow; also buffers ammonia.
- **Evidence: moderate.** Meta-analyses lean positive for extra reps-to-failure in resistance
  training; effect is **real but modest** — think an extra rep on later sets, not a transformation.
  Some trials are null. I'd rather tell you that than oversell it.
- **8 g of citrulline malate** (the 2:1 form used in most studies), or ~6 g pure L-citrulline.
- **60 minutes before** — plasma arginine peaks around then. So: with your 4pm meal.
- Buy it as a plain single-ingredient powder. It tastes sour. Zero sleep impact.

### Beta-alanine — 3.2–6.4 g/day, taken *daily*, not pre-workout
Commonly misunderstood: **this is not an acute supplement.** It works by raising muscle carnosine
over 4+ weeks, which buffers the H⁺ build-up that burns during longer sets.

- **Helps 60–240 second efforts** — so your 12–20 rep work (leg press, laterals, calf raises,
  leg extensions) benefits. It does **essentially nothing** for a 5-rep squat.
- **Evidence: good** for that specific duration window. Effect modest.
- Causes harmless tingling (paraesthesia) — split into 2 doses to reduce it.
- Taking it "pre-workout" does nothing. It's a daily loading supplement.

### Optional: dietary nitrate — beetroot juice, 2–3 h pre
6–8 mmol nitrate. Stronger evidence in endurance than lifting, but a genuine blood-flow effect and
it's a whole food. Take it with brunch rather than at 4pm. Skip if you're already on citrulline —
overlapping mechanism, and citrulline is the better-studied one for resistance training.

## 4. Caffeine: keep it, but move it

Here's the good news — **your existing habit is nearly right already.** Caffeine's half-life is
~5–6 h in most people (8+ h if you're a slow CYP1A2 metaboliser, which your sleep disruption
suggests you may be). That long tail is the problem, but it's also the opportunity:

**200 mg of caffeine, by the time you take it:**

| Taken | In your blood at **5:30pm gym** | At **12:30am bedtime** | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1:00pm** | 113 mg *(normal)* / 135 mg *(slow)* | 47 mg / 74 mg | ✅ **Do this** |
| 3:00pm | 146 / 161 mg | 60 / 88 mg | ⚠️ Borderline |
| 5:00pm | 188 / 192 mg | **78 / 104 mg** | ❌ This is what ruined your sleep |

**A 1pm coffee is still delivering an ergogenic dose at 5:30pm.** You get ~113 mg on board when
you start lifting — comfortably enough to matter — while leaving less than half as much at bedtime
as a 5pm scoop. 104 mg circulating at 12:30am is the equivalent of drinking a full coffee as you
get into bed. That's your answer.

### Your rules

1. **Hard cutoff: 1pm.** 2pm at the absolute latest. Nothing after.
2. **Cap at ~200 mg/day total** — one strong coffee, or two mild ones. You don't need 400 mg;
   most of the resistance-training benefit is present at 2–3 mg/kg.
3. **Keep it with brunch**, exactly as you do now. Just never move it later.
4. **Cycle it.** Use caffeine on **Legs (Wed) and Lower (Sat)** — your two hardest sessions — and
   go without on the other three. Daily use builds tolerance within 1–2 weeks and the effect
   fades; intermittent use keeps it potent and halves your weekly sleep exposure.
5. **If sleep is still disturbed at 1pm/200 mg, you're a slow metaboliser.** Drop to 100–150 mg,
   or move to 11am. Test it for a week and read the sleep log in the app.

### How much caffeine is actually in a coffee?

"One coffee" spans **50–300+ mg** — a sixfold range — which is why the cap above is in milligrams
rather than cups.

| What you're drinking | Caffeine |
|---|---|
| Instant, 1 **level** tsp (~2 g) | **60–80 mg** |
| Instant, 1 **heaped** tsp | **90–120 mg** |
| Espresso, single shot | **60–80 mg** |
| Espresso, **double** shot | **120–160 mg** |
| Café cappuccino / latte *(usually a double)* | **120–160 mg** |
| South Indian filter coffee, 1 tumbler | **50–100 mg** |
| Home French press / drip, 240 ml | **95–165 mg** |
| Starbucks grande brewed (473 ml) | **~310 mg** |
| Cold brew, 340 ml | **150–250 mg** |
| Black tea, 240 ml | 40–50 mg |
| Masala chai | 25–50 mg |
| Green tea | 25–35 mg |
| Red Bull 250 ml / Monster 500 ml | 80 / 160 mg |
| *A pre-workout scoop, for comparison* | *150–350 mg* |

> **Indian instant coffee is mostly robusta**, which carries roughly **twice** the caffeine of
> arabica (~2.2–2.7 % vs 1.2–1.5 % by weight). Assume Nescafé and Bru sit at the **high** end of
> the ranges above. Traditional filter coffee cuts the other way — the chicory in the blend has
> no caffeine and dilutes the dose.

**Rule of thumb: count shots and teaspoons, not cups.** ~70 mg per espresso shot, ~80 mg per level
teaspoon of instant. That figure is stable; "a cup" isn't.

**Your 200 mg spends as:** two heaped tsp of instant · *or* one café latte plus a tea later ·
*or* two filter coffees. **A single Starbucks grande brewed coffee (~310 mg) is over the cap on its
own** — more caffeine than the pre-workout you gave up, in the drink most people assume is harmless.

> **L-theanine (200 mg)** taken with caffeine smooths the jittery edge and is worth adding if you
> get anxious on it. Be clear on what it does *not* do: it has **no effect on caffeine's half-life**,
> so it does not help your sleep at all. Timing is the only thing that fixes that.

## 5. Don't bother

| Supplement | Why not |
|---|---|
| **BCAAs / EAAs** | Redundant at 165 g protein/day. You're already swimming in leucine. |
| **Arginine** | Poorly absorbed orally — largely destroyed by intestinal arginase. Citrulline is strictly better at raising arginine. |
| **Glutamine** | No performance benefit in fed, healthy lifters. |
| **"Test boosters"** | Nothing OTC meaningfully raises testosterone in a healthy 26-year-old. |
| **Mass gainers** | Sugar and maltodextrin at a large markup. Eat food. |
| **Proprietary "blends"** | Hide fairy-dust doses behind a total weight. If it won't state mg per ingredient, don't buy it. |
| **Yohimbine** | Jitteriness, anxiety, raised blood pressure. Not worth it. |

**Buy single ingredients.** Creatine monohydrate and citrulline malate as plain powders cost a
fraction of any branded pre-workout and you control the doses.

## 6. The whole protocol

**Daily**
- **5 g creatine monohydrate** — any time, every day including rest days.
- Optional: **3.2 g beta-alanine**, split into two doses.

**Training days (Mon/Tue/Wed/Fri/Sat)**
- **~12–1pm** — brunch. **Coffee here, ≤200 mg, never later than 1pm.**
  *(Optionally caffeine only on Wed and Sat.)*
- **~4:00pm** — the pre-workout meal: **40–60 g carbs + 20–25 g protein, low fat, low fibre.**
  Add **8 g citrulline malate** here.
- **~5:15pm** — optional banana or 4 dates if you feel flat.
- **5:30pm** — train. Water throughout; carbs sipped on Legs and Lower days.
- **~7:15pm** — dinner: protein + carbs.

**Total cost: creatine + citrulline powder.** No pre-workout tub, no sleep debt.

## 7. Two honest caveats

**Some days will still feel flat.** You sleep 6–7 hours. No protocol fixes that, and the
temptation will be to reach for a stimulant to force the session. Don't — that's precisely what
the [autoregulation rule](00-Program-Overview.md#6-autoregulation--mandatory-not-optional) exists
for. Log the bad sleep, take the cut-back session, keep the streak. That is the correct response,
not a failure.

**The biggest lever is still bedtime.** You finish training at 6:30pm and fall asleep at
midnight–1am. If you moved that to 11pm you'd gain an hour of sleep, and an hour of sleep will do
more for your training energy than every supplement on this page combined. Removing the 5pm
caffeine is what makes that shift possible in the first place — which is the real reason this
change matters.
