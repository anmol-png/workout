# Day 2 — PULL (Back · Rear Delts · Biceps)

**Target time:** ~60 min · **Working sets:** 18 · **Coverage:** back 10, rear delts 3, biceps 5

> Read [00-Program-Overview.md](00-Program-Overview.md) first — RPE, double progression and
> autoregulation are defined there and apply to every exercise below.

## Warm-up (~7 min)

| # | What | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 3 min easy bike or rower | Core temperature, blood flow |
| 2 | Band pull-aparts × 20 | Rear delts and mid-back — primes the muscles that should drive your rows |
| 3 | Dead hang from the bar × 20–30 s | Decompresses the spine, opens the lats, wakes up your grip |
| 4 | Scap pull-ups × 8 | Teaches you to initiate a pull from the shoulder blades rather than the arms |
| 5 | 1–2 light pulldown ramp sets | Grooves the pattern without fatigue |

## The one thing that determines whether this session works

**Pull with your back, not your arms.** The lats and mid-back are large, strong muscles; the
biceps are small. If the biceps initiate every rep, they become the limiting factor and your back
never gets close to a hard set. Every cue below is some version of this.

Think: **elbows drive back and down, shoulder blades move first, hands are just hooks.**

## A. Weighted Pull-up — *lats, vertical pull, relative strength*
**4 sets × 5–8 reps · RPE 8 · rest 2.5 min · Start: bodyweight**

> **If you can't do 6 clean bodyweight reps:** use the **Lat Pulldown** at 4 × 8–12 @ RPE 8
> instead, and progress it normally. Add pull-ups back in when your pulldown reaches roughly
> bodyweight for 8. Assisted pull-up machine also works.

- **Why first:** hardest and most demanding pull of the day, so it gets the freshest slot. It's
  also the best available builder of lat width — the muscle that most changes back appearance.
- **Why weighted / low reps:** treating the pull-up as a loadable strength lift (belt + plate)
  rather than an endurance test is what makes it progress. Rep-maxing bodyweight pull-ups plateaus
  fast; adding 2.5 kg does not.
- **Cues:** start from a full dead hang — no shortened range. Pull the elbows down toward your
  ribs, drive the chest to the bar. Squeeze at the top, control the descent over ~2 s. Avoid
  kipping or swinging entirely.
- **Grip:** pronated (overhand), slightly wider than shoulders. Not excessively wide — that
  shortens the range without adding lat recruitment.
- **Progression:** 4×8 clean → add **2.5 kg** on a dip belt, restart at 5.
- **Substitutes:** Lat Pulldown · Assisted Pull-up machine.

## B. Barbell Row — *mid-back thickness, the horizontal pull*
**3 sets × 8–12 reps · RPE 8 · rest 2 min · Start: ~40–45 kg**

- **Why:** pull-ups build **width** (lats, vertical plane); rows build **thickness** (rhomboids,
  mid-traps, rear delts, and the lats in their other function). A back trained only vertically is
  half-built, and horizontal pulling is also what balances all the pressing on Push day —
  directly protective for the shoulder joint.
- **Why 8–12 @ RPE 8:** hypertrophy range. RPE 8 matters here because the barbell row is a
  spinal-loading lift held in a hinged position — the last thing you want is form breaking down
  under a grinding final rep.
- **Cues:** hinge to roughly 45° or a bit lower, **back flat — never rounded**, brace hard. Pull
  the bar toward your lower ribs/upper stomach, elbows back and close-ish. Squeeze the shoulder
  blades together at the top. Control it down — don't let the plates yank you.
- **Common error:** rising up with the torso to heave the weight. If your torso angle changes
  during the set, the weight is too heavy.
- **Progression:** 3×12 clean → +2.5 kg, restart at 8.
- **Substitutes:** Chest-Supported Row (removes the lower-back demand entirely — a good choice on
  a low-recovery day) · Pendlay Row · Single-arm DB Row.

## C. Seated Cable Row, neutral grip — *mid-back, constant tension*
**3 sets × 10–12 reps · RPE 8–9 · rest 90 s · Start: calibrate**

- **Why a third back exercise:** the back is the largest muscle group in the upper body and needs
  the most volume — 16 sets/week here. It's also structurally diverse: lats, rhomboids, traps and
  rear delts all need work, and no single exercise covers them.
- **Why cable, why now:** the cable keeps tension constant through the whole range (unlike a
  barbell, which is easiest at the top) and it's supported, so you can push closer to failure than
  the barbell row without spinal cost. Neutral grip is the most shoulder-friendly.
- **Cues:** don't lean back past vertical to complete the rep. Let the shoulder blades **protract
  fully** at the front for a real stretch on the lats, then retract hard on the pull. Elbows in.
- **Progression:** 3×12 @ RPE ≤ 8 → next notch, restart at 10.
- **Substitutes:** Chest-Supported Machine Row · Single-arm cable row.

## D. Superset — *rear delts + biceps*
Do **D1 → 20 s → D2 → rest 75–90 s → repeat. 3 rounds.**

### D1. Reverse Pec Deck (or Face Pull) — *rear delts*
**3 × 15–20 · RPE 9 · Start: light, calibrate**
- **Why it gets dedicated work:** rear delts are hit as a secondary in every row, but they're small,
  they recover fast, and they're chronically underdeveloped in anyone who presses. They're also the
  primary counterweight to all the internal-rotation work on Push day — **the single best insurance
  against shoulder problems** in a program with this much pressing.
- **Why 15–20 reps:** small muscle, tiny leverage. Heavy loads just recruit the traps and mid-back
  instead. High reps with strict form is the only way to actually load a rear delt.
- **Cues:** think about pulling with the **elbows**, not the hands. Slight bend in the arm, held
  constant. Squeeze at the end range, control back. If you feel it in your traps, go lighter.

### D2. Incline Dumbbell Curl — *biceps, in the stretched position*
**3 × 8–12 · RPE 9 · Start: 2 × 8 kg**
- **Why incline specifically:** lying back on an incline bench puts your upper arms **behind** your
  torso, which lengthens the biceps' long head. Training a muscle under stretch is a strong
  hypertrophy stimulus, and this is the easiest way to get the biceps there. A standing curl never
  reaches that position.
- **Cues:** upper arms stay back and still — don't let the elbows drift forward, that turns it into
  a front raise. Full extension at the bottom (this is the whole point), full supination at the
  top. Slow negative.

## E. Cable Rope Hammer Curl — *brachialis + forearm, finisher*
**2 sets × 12–15 · RPE 9 · rest 60 s · Start: calibrate**
- **Why:** the neutral (hammer) grip shifts load onto the **brachialis** — a muscle that sits
  *underneath* the biceps. Developing it pushes the biceps up and adds visible arm thickness, and
  it also builds the brachioradialis, which improves grip on every pull.
- **Why 2 sets:** the biceps have already done 4 sets of pull-ups, 6 sets of rows and 3 sets of
  curls as movers or assistants. This is a targeted finisher, not a fourth exercise.
- **Cues:** elbows fixed at your sides, no swinging. Squeeze hard at the top.

## Session summary

| | Exercise | Sets × Reps | RPE | Rest | Start |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | Weighted Pull-up *(or Lat Pulldown)* | 4 × 5–8 | 8 | 2.5 min | bodyweight |
| B | Barbell Row | 3 × 8–12 | 8 | 2 min | ~40–45 kg |
| C | Seated Cable Row (neutral) | 3 × 10–12 | 8–9 | 90 s | calibrate |
| D1 | Reverse Pec Deck / Face Pull | 3 × 15–20 | 9 | superset | light |

| E | Cable Rope Hammer Curl | 2 × 12–15 | 9 | 60 s | calibrate |

**Grip failing before your back?** Use straps on A and B. Straps are not cheating — they stop a
small muscle from capping the stimulus to a large one. Train grip separately if you care about it.

**Bad sleep (< 6 h) or readiness ≤ 2/5?** Drop the last set of everything, subtract 1 from every
RPE target. See [Autoregulation](00-Program-Overview.md#6-autoregulation--mandatory-not-optional).
